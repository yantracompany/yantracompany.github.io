Stanford AI Product Playbook

The Stanford HAI studio blends research-grade rigor with an unapologetically product-minded cadence. This field note series captures what their teams actually do between reviews so you can mirror the habits without recreating the university setting.

Experimentation rituals that travel well

Stanford teams work in four-week hypothesis sprints anchored around a shared lab log. Each log documents the prompt stack, data boundaries, open risks, and success criteria, then ends with a policy reflection section. We brought the template into our own tooling and found three moves especially sticky.

First, host the prompt recipe wall inside your existing doc stack so everyone contributes in real time. Second, send a Monday lab note that highlights regressions and carries action owners forward; it keeps legal, policy, and product design aligned. Third, invite your responsible innovation or compliance partners into demo days early. They ask the uncomfortable questions when it still matters.

The quiet discipline of experience guardrails

The star of the Stanford workflow is the ethics story card. It is a one-page dossier that travels with every UX flow. The card captures the user scenario, potential harm, planned mitigation, and the telemetry probe that will confirm whether the mitigation worked. We attach the card directly to Figma frames and ticket descriptions so the conversation never drifts back to abstract statements about principles.

To make the practice stick, we run short office hours every Thursday where PMs and designers can co-author cards with the policy team. By Friday, the cards are reviewed alongside the latest prototypes and added to the design history archive. When leadership asks how we vetted a flow, the evidence is already bundled.

Outcome telemetry, not vanity dashboards

Instead of focusing on click-through rates or MAUs, Stanford labs track whether their assistants reduced toil, increased trust, or helped someone make a better decision. We adopted the same “North Star wall” concept and wired three signals into our analytics suite: decision delta, time saved, and trust nudges. The wall sits next to the roadmap so the conversation is about outcomes, not busy charts.

Every dashboard callout lists the owner responsible for the signal. During fortnightly reviews we read the telemetry next to the ethics story cards to understand whether the mitigations we promised are landing. That rhythm has become a forcing function for investment debates and a subtle trust-building mechanism with leadership.
