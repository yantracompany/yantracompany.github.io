Research Brief / GenAI Signals

Curious Findings in Recent GenAI Research

Generative AI research is moving quickly, and along the way, researchers have uncovered surprising and sometimes counter-intuitive patterns. From creativity studies to environmental concerns, here are the findings shaping the conversation today.

Signal 01 - Human + GenAI vs. GenAI alone

A meta-analysis of more than 8,200 participants found that GenAI alone performs similarly to humans on creative tasks, but when humans collaborate with GenAI the results are significantly better. The trade-off—diversity of ideas drops when humans and AI generate together, leading to more convergent outputs and fewer surprising directions.

Playbook move

Introduce rotating persona prompts or multi-agent voting steps so each sprint keeps a healthy dose of divergent ideas before synthesis.

Signal 02 - Persona stacks re-open idea space

To address the narrowing, researchers experimented with multiple AI personas—models simulating different backgrounds or preferences. When humans worked with these varied prompts, the resulting outputs regained or even surpassed the diversity of purely human ideation. In other words, diversity in prompts plus diversity in outcomes.

  • Run back-to-back ideation passes with contrasting personas.
  • Score each pass for novelty before merging.
  • Log persona and outcome pairings to build an institutional prompt graph.

Signal 03 - Academia normalizes GenAI

In just one year, the percentage of computing students who reported never using ChatGPT fell from 34% to 6%. GenAI is rapidly becoming normalized in education. Interestingly, frequent daily use dipped slightly, hinting that students now use AI more strategically rather than habitually.

Signal 04 - Trust varies by task

Large-scale surveys show researchers are far more comfortable using GenAI for language editing or data analysis than for experiment design or peer review. Junior researchers are often more open to broader uses, while senior academics and non-technical fields remain cautious.

Tasks with high trust

Language edits, summarisation, accessible formatting, code linting.

Tasks with low trust

Novel experimental design, peer review, compliance evidence, legal risk scoring.

Signal 05 - Ethics, disclosure, transparency

Across fields, concerns about transparency, reproducibility, bias, and hallucinations remain top of mind. Many papers argue disclosure of how and when GenAI was used should become standard practice, but one-size-fits-all policies fall short—discipline-specific norms will be essential.

  1. Create a short disclosure rubric (purpose, prompts, human review checkpoints).
  2. Pair it with reproducibility notes (model version, sampling parameters, toolchain).
  3. Log hallucination and bias incidents in the same telemetry system as general defects.

Signal 06 - Sustainability telemetry

Training, fine-tuning, and deploying large models comes with significant energy and water costs. Data centers powering GenAI consume increasing electricity while cooling and hardware manufacturing add to the environmental load. Researchers are calling for more efficient architectures and transparent reporting of AI's footprint.

Ethics & sustainability

Track prompts per watt-hour, auto-flag long-running inference chains, and surface greener blueprint suggestions inside governance reviews.

Field notes to socialize internally

Each of these findings reminds us that GenAI is both a powerful collaborator and a disruptive force. It can amplify creativity but also homogenize it; democratize access but also raise ethical and environmental questions. Staying curious about these nuances helps teams shape AI into a tool that serves people more thoughtfully.

"Generative AI does not just change how we create—it changes what we value in creativity, collaboration, and sustainability."
