Sustainable AI Field Notes

Stanford’s sustainability partnerships program has been quietly prototyping a low-carbon AI stack. We spent time with the practitioners and walked away with a set of routines that feel applicable even outside campus.

Observability that treats carbon like latency

The Stanford teams route GPU hours, cooling water, and carbon mix into the same observability system that already tracks latency and error budgets. Each workload gets a fact sheet that shows kilowatt hours per inference batch, liters of water per training job, and the carbon intensity of the hosting region over the past week.

You do not need a new platform to follow suit. Add derived metrics in your APM tool, appoint owners for the signals, and run a weekly stand-up that includes infra, finance, and sustainability leads. Spikes get triaged with the same urgency as a P1 incident, and remediation steps are documented in the runbook.

Procurement scorecards that carry real weight

Every Stanford RFP ships with a sustainability appendix. It asks whether renewable bundles are attached to GPU leases, whether hardware recycling SLAs are signed, and whether cooling practices are disclosed. Suppliers that meet the bar earn preferred status; everyone else receives a time-boxed remediation plan.

We re-created the scorecard in a shared spreadsheet and treat it as a living document. Procurement updates the status column, responsible AI leads contribute suggested clauses, and executives can see which partners are lagging without digging through email threads.

Impact storytelling that unlocks sponsorship

Data alone rarely moves senior leaders. The Stanford group builds a one-page impact board that pairs telemetry with narrative examples: a model optimization that saved megawatt hours and improved latency, or a supplier negotiation that bundled carbon-neutral power with additional GPUs. We adopted the same format and present it at quarterly business reviews so sustainability sits alongside growth and reliability.
